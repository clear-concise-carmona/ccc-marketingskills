## 3. Custom order instructions (preserve these exactly)

- Choose Your Word items: pick a word from the dropdown. For a word not on the list, the site says to select the custom option, complete checkout, then email aliensofbrooklyn@gmail.com or DM @aliensofbrooklyn with the word. `[SITE]` The custom option exists on beanies but not on caps as of 2026-09-14 (see section 2). `[CLIENT REVIEW]`
- Custom Order Cap and Custom Shirt Orders: complete the purchase, then email the exact image or phrase to aliensofbrooklyn@gmail.com. `[SITE]`
- Turnaround stated on the site: custom caps "about 5 to 7 days"; custom beanies "1 to 2 weeks"; custom shirts "one week to be shipped out." `[SITE]`
- "Custom word and custom image items are made for you and are final sale." `[SITE]`
- In-store custom printing at the East Village location: bring an image, choose a shirt, hoodie, or tote, printed "in under 30 minutes." The 30-minute promise applies to walk-ins only. Customers with their own garment or special materials are told to email before visiting. Pricing is not on the page. `[SITE]`
- The site does not yet have a text field for the custom word on the product page. `[ASSUMPTION]` that the email step is temporary; treat the current flow as the truth until the owners change it.

## 5. Target audiences

From the owner brand brief. Not published on the site. Use for targeting and tone decisions; do not describe customers in public copy in ways that single out a protected class.

- Primary: LGBTQIA+ creatives and professionals, roughly 22 to 32, in East Austin, Brooklyn, and urban California. Values community, authenticity, self-expression, small business support. `[BRIEF]`
- Secondary: festival and live-music regulars, roughly 25 to 35, buying statement pieces for events and social content. `[BRIEF]`
- Tertiary: nostalgic millennials, roughly 28 to 40, higher order value, quality-focused, repeat-purchase potential. `[BRIEF]`
- Geographic priority in the brief: New York and Brooklyn first, then California, Texas and Austin, New Jersey, Florida. `[BRIEF]` `[ASSUMPTION]` that the two physical stores make NYC and Austin the practical priority today.

## 8. Brand voice

From the owner brand brief and confirmed by the site's product names and FAQ tone.

- Primary tone: fun, cheeky, playful, inclusive. Secondary: bold, confident, nostalgic, authentic. `[BRIEF]`
- Short, punchy sentences. Casual, conversational. Puns and 90s/Y2K references (dial-up, TRL, Y2K panic, flip phones). `[BRIEF]`
- Space and alien metaphors in moderation: "beam aboard," "mothership," "intergalactic." `[BRIEF]`
- Inclusive language by default: they/them where a pronoun is needed, no assumptions about who is buying for whom. `[BRIEF]`
- Emoji: one or two per message at most (alien, UFO, sparkle). None in policy, FAQ, or legal text. `[BRIEF]`
- Never corporate, never timid, never over-apologetic, never gatekeeping. `[BRIEF]`
- No em-dashes in any copy. Use periods, commas, or colons. (Owner writing rule.)
- Provocative or explicit slogans: only the ones already on the site or approved in writing by the owners. See `docs/claims-and-disclaimers.md`.

Reference copy from the brief (approved examples of the voice, not mandatory lines):
- "Serving looks from another dimension"
- "90s kids, assemble!"
- "Your 90s nostalgia called. It's waiting in your cart."

## 9. Visual and style notes

The brand's visual system is in transition and three internal documents describe different palettes (a beige/navy legacy system, a near-black/neon green/magenta system, and a NEO Y2K acid lime/electric indigo direction). `[CLIENT REVIEW]` Confirm the current system with the owners before any design or template work. ## 12. Marketing skill usage notes for this brand

- `product-marketing`: the context file at `.agents/product-marketing.md` is already populated; update it there, not here, when positioning changes.
- `copywriting` and `copy-editing`: preserve every product fact in section 2 to 4. Rewrite the hook and the vibe, not the materials, sizes, turnaround, or policies.
- `cro` and `popups`: the two flows that matter most are product page to add-to-cart on the Choose Your Word items and the custom order submission step.
- `seo-audit`, `schema`, `ai-seo`: collection and product pages are the landing pages for category keywords ("custom word dad hat," "custom word beanie," "y2k streetwear nyc," "y2k clothing austin"). Store pages carry the local signals.
- `emails` and `sms`: welcome, abandoned checkout, post-purchase (custom items need a "what happens next" message), back-in-stock, and win-back. Custom items are final sale; say so in post-purchase copy.
- `social`: Instagram first, TikTok second. Product posts need price and a way to buy; store posts need the address.
- `ads` and `ad-creative`: explicit menu words and celebrity-named products constrain what can run on Meta and TikTok. Draft with the clean subset and flag the rest.
- `influencer-marketing`: gifting first, micro-creators in NYC and Austin, FTC disclosure on every post.
- `analytics`: aggregated or sanitized numbers only. No exports in the repo.
- `launch`: drops, collaborations, seasonal capsules, and store events. "Soft opening" language only until the owners say otherwise.
- `competitor-profiling`, `customer-research`: study patterns, never copy competitor wording.
