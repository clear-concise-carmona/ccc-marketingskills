# Claims and Disclaimers for Aliens of Brooklyn Marketing Copy

What agents may say about the brand and its products, what needs a source first, what needs owner approval, and what to avoid. This applies to product pages, collections, homepage, email, SMS, social, ads, press materials, and structured data.

Nothing here is legal advice. When a question is about trademark, likeness, advertising law, or platform policy, flag it for the owners to take to counsel.

## 1. Approved claim types (with the source in hand)

These may be used as long as the current product page or FAQ still says them. Re-check the live page before publishing.

- The FAQ self-description: "queer-owned 90s and Y2K pop culture streetwear brand born in Crown Heights, Brooklyn in 2012."
- Two store locations at the addresses printed on the site.
- "Made in New York City" for Choose Your Word caps and beanies, as their product pages and the FAQ state. Custom shirts are not covered: the only origin wording for them is in a meta description written during the September 2026 SEO pass, so that claim is `[CLIENT REVIEW]` (section 3).
- Materials and construction stated on the specific product page (for example, 100% cotton caps; 100% acrylic beanies; Comfort Colors 1717 tees at 100% ring-spun US cotton, 6.1 oz; custom shirt 50/25/25 poly, cotton, rayon blend).
- Sizing as the FAQ lists it.
- The custom order process as the live page states it (check out, then email or DM the word or image). Do not reference a "custom option" on cap pages until the owners add it to the dropdown (see `.agents/aliens-of-brooklyn-brand-context.md` section 2).
- Turnaround exactly as stated: custom caps about 5 to 7 days, custom beanies 1 to 2 weeks, custom shirts about one week to ship.
- Shipping policy text and the returns policy text, quoted or paraphrased without softening.
- In-store custom printing at the East Village location in under 30 minutes for walk-ins.
- The Kinkysweat collaboration on the Celeb Beanies line.
- Menu words that appear in the live product dropdowns.

## 2. Claims requiring source verification before use

Find the page or owner document first, then cite it.

- Any price (they change; quote the live page).
- Materials for products whose pages do not list them (sweatshirts, totes, celeb beanies, baby tees, mugs, magnets).
- Care instructions beyond what a page states.
- Whether a collection is in stock.
- Which sales channels (Instagram Shop, TikTok Shop, Google Shopping) are live and approved.
- Whether any discount code, free shipping threshold, or loyalty program exists.
- Whether reviews exist anywhere the brand can quote them.

## 3. Claims requiring owner review before publishing

- Any origin or made-in claim for custom shirts, totes, sweatshirts, or other products whose visible page does not state one.
- Founder name, biography, hometown, and the pop-up and store history.
- Identity descriptors beyond "queer-owned" (for example, descriptors that appear only in press).
- Store hours, opening dates, and event details.
- "Grand opening" or any opening language other than "soft opening."
- Tagline and branded CTAs from the brand brief appearing on the site.
- Press mentions, quotes, awards, or logos.
- Any partner, venue, artist, festival, or creator name other than Kinkysweat.
- Sustainability, ethical sourcing, labor, or "eco" claims of any kind. None are on the site today.
- Any statement about who the customers are.

## 4. Claims to avoid entirely

- Celebrity endorsement, partnership, approval, or "official" merchandise status. Product titles that reference public figures are the owners' existing catalog decisions; do not extend them, and never write copy that implies the person is involved.
- "Licensed," "authentic [franchise]," "official," or "authorized" in relation to any third-party name, character, show, film, artist, or team.
- Health, wellness, or body-related claims. No copy that shames, ranks, or excludes bodies, and no "flattering for" language tied to body type.
- Guarantees: "guaranteed delivery," "guaranteed fit," "satisfaction guaranteed," "risk-free."
- Superlatives without evidence: "best," "number one," "top-rated," "award-winning," "as seen in."
- Fake social proof: invented reviews, star ratings, customer counts, "X people bought this today," fabricated testimonials.
- Fake urgency: countdowns or "only X left" that are not driven by real inventory.
- Invented manufacturing or sourcing details: fabric mills, print methods, or origin claims not on the page.
- Copy that suggests counterfeit or replica goods, or unauthorized use of anyone's likeness.
- Copy that copies a competitor's wording.

## 5. Product material claims

- Quote the product page. If the page names a blank (for example, Comfort Colors 1717), the blank's published specs may be restated for that product only.
- No "premium," "luxury," or "heavyweight" unless the spec supports it (6.1 oz garment-dyed cotton may be called heavyweight; the page says so).
- Custom shirt blend and image area: use the page's numbers.
- Care: only what the page states. Owner approval for anything new.

## 6. Made-in and location claims

- "Made in New York City" applies to the products whose pages say it. Do not extend it to the whole catalog. Several tees are on third-party blanks; the brand's work is the print and the design.
- Acceptable framing for the brand as a whole: "designed in Brooklyn" only with owner confirmation; "born in Crown Heights, Brooklyn" is on the FAQ and is fine.
- Addresses: print them exactly as the site does. Neighborhood names (East Village, East Austin) are on the site and are fine.
- Do not reference former locations named in older internal materials in customer-facing copy unless the owners ask for the history angle and confirm the details.

## 7. Shipping and fulfillment claims

- Use the policy text: ships within one week of purchase, shipping calculated by weight, international costs extra.
- Do not name carriers, promise delivery dates, or state "free shipping" without owner confirmation.
- Custom items: state the production window, then the shipping window, as two separate steps.
- Post-purchase copy for custom items must include the email-your-word step if the buyer chose the custom option.

## 8. Custom order claims

- Character limits, font choices, thread colors, and image requirements are not published. Do not state them. Direct the buyer to email or DM.
- Final sale on custom word and custom image items. Say it before checkout and again after.
- Do not promise that any word or image will be accepted. The owners may decline requests. Phrase as "send us your word and we'll confirm."
- In-store printing: the 30-minute promise is for walk-ins with a ready image, at the NYC store only. Pricing is not published.
- Custom orders for kids: the site states hats fit ages 6 and up. Nothing more.

## 9. Celebrity, pop culture, and trademark references

- The catalog uses pop culture references in product names. Agents do not create new references, rename products, or add new public figures, characters, franchises, lyrics, logos, or artwork.
- Copy about an existing product may repeat the product title. It may not add the person's image, quote them, tag their account, or suggest association or approval.
- Meme formats and cultural references in the voice (dial-up, TRL, Y2K panic) are fine. Song lyrics, film lines, and logos are not.
- Hashtags with a celebrity or franchise name are an owner decision.
- "Keep Austin Weird" is a phrase associated with a trademark held by others; the brand's own line is "Keep Austin Alien." Use the brand's line.
- When in doubt, write the draft without the reference and list the reference as an option for the owners.

## 10. Influencer, creator, and UGC claims

- Every gifted or paid post needs a clear disclosure per FTC guidance and platform rules. Put it in the brief; do not leave it to the creator.
- Do not describe a creator as a partner, ambassador, or collaborator without a written agreement the owners confirm.
- Reposting customer photos and videos requires permission and credit.
- Do not edit a review or testimonial beyond trimming for length, and never invent one.
- Incentivized reviews must be disclosed as such.

## 11. Privacy and customer data

- No real customer names, handles, emails, phone numbers, addresses, order numbers, or purchase histories in any draft, example, screenshot, or commit.
- Placeholders: "Alex Example," "alex@example.com," "order #1001," "{{FIRST_NAME}}."
- Aggregated numbers supplied by the owners may appear in an internal deliverable, never in public copy.
- SMS and email drafts assume consent has been collected by the platform; they do not assert it.
- No credentials, tokens, or account IDs anywhere in this repo.

## 12. Client review checklist before publishing

Copy this into the handoff summary and check each line.

- [ ] Every product fact matches the live product page or FAQ on the review date.
- [ ] Every price was re-checked on the live page.
- [ ] Custom order steps and turnaround are unchanged and complete.
- [ ] "Final sale" appears wherever custom items are sold or confirmed.
- [ ] Shipping and returns language matches the policies; no exchanges or free returns implied.
- [ ] Made-in claims appear only on products whose pages state them.
- [ ] No new celebrity, character, franchise, artist, festival, venue, or partner names.
- [ ] No endorsement, "official," "licensed," or "authentic" wording tied to third parties.
- [ ] No discount, code, or threshold unless the owners confirmed it is live.
- [ ] No store hours, event dates, or "grand opening" language unless confirmed.
- [ ] No superlatives, guarantees, fake urgency, or invented social proof.
- [ ] No body, health, or protected-class language in copy or targeting.
- [ ] Disclosure language included for any creator or incentivized content.
- [ ] No customer data, credentials, or internal metrics in the copy or the repo.
- [ ] Every `[CLIENT REVIEW]` and `[ASSUMPTION]` tag is listed in the handoff summary.
- [ ] No em-dashes; brand voice rules from `.agents/aliens-of-brooklyn-brand-context.md` followed.
- [ ] `scripts/validate-agent-docs.sh` passes.
